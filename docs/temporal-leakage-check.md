## Question

The 27-day risks that the simulation uses are produced by dealing the days to 5 folds round robin. A day is predicted by a model that was **also trained on the days after it**. The day's own rows never enter the training data, but information from future days does. In real use the model sees only the past.

## Method

The first 7 days are used for training only; the following days are predicted in blocks of 4 days, and for every block the model is trained **on earlier days only** (a window that grows forward). The same rows as with the round-robin folds (81,818 appointments, 20 days) are compared.

## Conclusion

The AUC difference is very small: the round-robin folds do not noticeably inflate the results. The decided setting gains +2.81 and +2.88 patients with the two methods; the difference in waiting is +0.89 and +0.91 min. Two further points: (1) there are only 27 days, so the first days are predicted with little training data; (2) both methods compute the patient history features from records before the appointment date (`ml/data/clean_data.py`), so the features themselves do not leak.
